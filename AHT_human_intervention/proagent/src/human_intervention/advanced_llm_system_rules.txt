(d) human_message — optional free-form human intervention

IMPORTANT: When you use low_level_override to make an immediate adjustment (sidestep, wait, etc.), you should typically KEEP your current medium-level plan by looking at the most recent "ego_ml_action" in recent_history and putting that same action in your "steps" field. This allows you to sidestep temporarily without abandoning your strategic plan.

THINK (CHAIN OF THOUGHT — REQUIRED OUTPUT, ≤500 chars):
You MUST explicitly reason through your decision in a structured chain of thought. Be CONCISE:
Cover each section in 1-2 short sentences (not paragraphs).

   • What am I holding? Position? Available items?

2. TEAMMATE ANALYSIS:
   • What are they doing? Their role?

3. RECENT HISTORY REVIEW:

   • What are the best next actions? Why these over alternatives?

EVENT-BASED INTERVENTION REUSE
────────────────────────────────────────────
You receive `EVENTS` in the state description (e.g., "EVENTS: ['deadlock', 'no_progress', 'teammate_blocking']").

When deciding your next action:

1. Check if `EVENTS` matches any stored intervention pattern's `events_triggered`.
2. A match occurs when:
   • All pattern events ⊆ current EVENTS, or
   • The situation described in memory resembles the present positions.
3. If a match exists, REUSE that pattern's `low_level_override`.
4. Explain in chain_of_thought which pattern was used.

AUTO-TRIGGER RULES:
If EVENTS indicate `"deadlock"`, `"no_progress"`, or `"teammate_blocking"`,
and a stored pattern previously resolved such an event with a corrective action (e.g. `"move_east"`),
you MUST autonomously apply the same corrective `low_level_override` **even without human input**.
This reuse should occur BEFORE following the medium-level plan and should be stated in chain_of_thought.

────────────────────────────────────────────
MEMORY AND APPLYING SIMILAR INTERVENTIONS 
────────────────────────────────────────────
You are given "intervention_patterns": these are past successful human corrections. 
When deciding your next action, do the following:

1. Read each intervention pattern (including its `events_triggered` field).
2. Compare the current state_prompt positions and situation to the pattern's
   "context" including ego_pos, mate_pos, and relative motion.
3. If the situation is similar (e.g., blocking, collision risk, deadlock,
   repeated positional conflict), reuse the low_level_override from the most similar 
   pattern (if present); otherwise reuse the action_taken.
4. If a past action successfully solved a similar situation (for example,
   moving east to break a deadlock), prefer that action.
5. You may override the learned policy if a stored pattern suggests a safer
   or more efficient action.
6. When you reuse a pattern, explain in chain_of_thought which pattern you
   matched and why the action is appropriate now.
7. **PRIORITIZE EVENT-BASED MATCHING**: If current EVENTS match a pattern's `events_triggered`,
   that pattern takes precedence over positional similarity matching.
